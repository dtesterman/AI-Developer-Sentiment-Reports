## Headline
AI coding tools just became infrastructure — and the bill came due

## Body
Two stories landed in the same five days and reframed how teams think about AI coding assistants. On **June 1**, GitHub flipped [Copilot to usage-based billing](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/) — same headline prices, but bundled allotment replaced with a monthly **"AI Credits"** pool that meters every token. Within five days, [Cursor responded by *cutting* prices and adding enterprise spend controls](https://thenewstack.io/cursor-pricing-token-billing/), explicitly framed as a **"tokenomics reckoning"**. [Simon Willison reported that Uber now caps coding agents at **$1,500 per employee per tool per month**](https://bsky.app/profile/simonwillison.net/post/3mnf2w4ctnc2n) — the first concrete enterprise spend cap of the year.

The other story: **two of the four major AI coding tools went down 48 hours apart**. [Microsoft Copilot fell over on June 1](https://windowsnews.ai/article/microsoft-copilot-outage-june-1-2026-reliability-and-ai-workflow-risk.421251) — the very day its billing change took effect. On **June 2**, [Anthropic logged elevated errors on Opus 4.6](https://status.claude.com/) for roughly **5 hours and 45 minutes**, taking out Claude, the API, and Claude Code. [Thoughtworks called it directly](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/blog/generative-ai/claude-outage-june-2026): "AI's increasing status as infrastructure."

## Why this matters
Two of the four dominant agentic IDE vendors restructured billing in the same week, in opposite directions — and two of them lost service availability at the worst possible time. For procurement, the cost line is no longer per-seat; for SREs, model providers are now load-bearing dependencies. **Watch for:** an Anthropic public post-mortem of the June 2 incident, and week-2 (Jun 8–15) practitioner reaction once Copilot users see their first AI Credits bill.

## Headline
Reviewing AI code now costs more than writing it

## Body

Three reports landed this week saying the same thing in different ways: the bottleneck has moved. [A Harness survey](https://sdtimes.com/softwaredev/the-invisible-burden-how-ai-is-redefining-developer-productivity-in-2026/) of engineering leaders found **81%** are spending more time on code review since AI rolled out — and AI-written pull requests now wait **4.6×** longer to get picked up, but are reviewed **2×** faster once they are. [Stack Overflow calls it "decision fatigue"](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/05/21/coding-agents-are-giving-everyone-decision-fatigue/): the cost isn't typing time, it's the constant trust-or-override choices that wear engineers out by mid-afternoon. [Thoughtworks named the failure mode](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/radar/techniques/complacency-with-ai-generated-code) — "complacency with AI-generated code" — and put it on the *do-not-do* list of their May radar. [Addy Osmani published a working framework](https://addyo.substack.com/p/code-review-in-the-age-of-ai) for teams whose review backlog is now half AI.

## Why this matters

Every productivity claim about AI coding has assumed review costs stay flat. They don't. If the Harness **4.6×/2×** numbers replicate, the net productivity case shifts from *obviously positive* to *it depends on your review culture*. Watch for: a second survey to corroborate or disconfirm.

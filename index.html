Over 11 weeks of tracking AI coding tools, the conversation has shifted five times — from "is the code any good?" to "is the agent safe?" to "is the toolchain right?" to "who governs the gate?" and now this week to "who pays the review cost?" The story keeps moving from the artifact to the practice.
This week’s big story
Reviewing AI code now costs more than writing it
Three reports landed this week saying the same thing in different ways: the bottleneck has moved. A Harness survey of engineering leaders found 81% are spending more time on code review since AI rolled out — and AI-written pull requests now wait 4.6× longer to get picked up, but are reviewed 2× faster once they are. Stack Overflow calls it "decision fatigue": the cost isn't typing time, it's the constant trust-or-override choices that wear engineers out by mid-afternoon. Thoughtworks named the failure mode — "complacency with AI-generated code" — and put it on the do-not-do list of their May radar. Addy Osmani published a working framework for teams whose review backlog is now half AI.
Every productivity claim about AI coding has assumed review costs stay flat. They don't. If the Harness 4.6×/2× numbers replicate, the net productivity case shifts from obviously positive to it depends on your review culture. Watch for: a second survey to corroborate or disconfirm.

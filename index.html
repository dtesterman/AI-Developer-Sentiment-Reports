TL;DR  ·  The story so far
Across fifteen weeks of tracking, the developer-AI conversation has moved from "is the code any good?" through "is the agent safe?" "who governs the gate?" and "who pays?" — and this week it crystallizes into "the MCP supply chain IS the incident class, and BYOK is the economic counter-move." The story that ties the arc together: AI coding is now infrastructure with both an attack surface and an architecture decision that teams must own.
This week’s big story
A leaked Sentry key can now hijack your AI coding agent — and VS Code just changed the math
On June 21, The New Stack disclosed "AgentJacking" — a new exploit class where a single exposed Sentry API key is enough to take over Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex through their tool-connection layer. It joins five Critical or Significant in-window incidents including OX Security's "Mother of All AI Supply Chains" disclosure (which estimates ~200,000 vulnerable instances across 150 million downloads), Check Point's CVE-2025-59536 for Claude Code project files, and a coordinated 15-plugin malicious JetBrains campaign. The MCP protocol that ties AI agents to your tools is now the single most damaging attack surface in AI coding.
In the same seven days, the economic floor moved. On June 18, VS Code shipped BYOK — developers can now plug their own Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, or Ollama key directly into the editor without a Copilot subscription. Databricks Agent Bricks makes MCP a first-class Unity Catalog citizen with the deterministic permission layer AgentJacking exploits. And Pragmatic Engineer's "Pulse" formalizes that engineering departments are visibly cutting AI tooling spend — Microsoft cancelled the majority of its internal Claude Code licenses, Uber exhausted its full-year AI budget by April, and Cursor repackaged its Teams pricing defensively.
Two structural shifts landed in the same week. The MCP layer — the connective tissue making "agentic coding" possible — is now confirmed as the dominant attack surface, with 5 incidents in one week. And the BYOK release means teams no longer have to buy access at all — they can bring their own key. Together, they reframe AI coding as something to architect for, not just buy. Watch for: Anthropic's response to AgentJacking (no advisory in window), the first production case study of VS Code BYOK at enterprise scale, and resolution of the Cursor acquisition concern that drew 427 likes and 210 replies on r/cursor.
